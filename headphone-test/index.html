A second listening check

This channel test answers a specific question: does the intended left or right cue reach you? For a longer listening exercise, this video provides another route to test your headphones.

The player is provided by YouTube and may set or receive data under YouTube’s policies. Comfort, fit, noise isolation, battery life and build quality still need to be assessed in your normal use—not inferred from a browser test.

How to use this test

  1. Set a comfortable volume

    Start low and adjust your device volume before playing a prompt. Do not use the test to listen at an uncomfortable level.

  2. Play left and right

    Use the separate left and right controls, listening for each cue in the expected ear or speaker.

  3. Play both channels

    Use the both-channel prompt to compare the combined output with the separate cues.

  4. Confirm and stop

    Mark what you heard and use Stop at any time. The page records your confirmation instead of assigning an automatic pass.

What we check

Audio begins only after your action, which avoids browser autoplay restrictions and gives you control over when sound starts. This online headphone, headset, earphone and earbud test sends short, controlled prompts to the left, right, or both channels where the browser and output route preserve that channel information.

The page asks you to confirm the cue you heard because web pages generally cannot inspect the physical headphones, speakers, volume setting, or route chosen by the operating system. A browser playback event alone is not evidence that sound reached your ears.

Short ramps at the beginning and end of a prompt can reduce abrupt clicks. The tool is for channel identification and practical setup checks, not a measurement of audio fidelity.

What this test can’t tell you

This is not a hearing test, a speaker-health diagnosis, a surround-sound verification, a noise-cancelling test, a sound-quality test, or a calibrated frequency-response measurement.

Bluetooth routing, operating-system balance settings, adapters, mono audio, browser output choice and physical fit can all change what you hear without being visible to this page.

Something not working?

There is no sound

Check device volume, browser tab mute, the selected output device and any inline remote or headset controls. Start playback again only after adjusting volume.

Both prompts play on one side

Check operating-system accessibility and audio-balance settings, then reconnect the headset or adapter. Try another audio source to isolate the route.

Left and right seem reversed

Confirm the headset orientation and cable/adaptor connections. The page can label the sent channel, but it cannot identify where a physical wiring change occurred.

The sound starts late or cuts out

Bluetooth devices may take time to wake or change profile. Pause, reconnect the device, and try again with other audio apps closed.

Common questions

Can I use this online headphone test with a headset, earphones or earbuds?

Yes. The page plays its prompts through the output device your browser is using, whether that is headphones, a headset, earphones, earbuds or speakers. It cannot identify the physical device automatically.

Can the page tell whether I heard the tone?

No. It can send a prompt; your confirmation is the meaningful result.

Is this a hearing test?

No. It does not use calibrated equipment or controlled listening conditions and cannot assess hearing.

Can it test noise cancelling or sound quality?

No. The page does not measure noise cancellation, frequency response or overall sound quality. It is a practical audio and left/right channel check.

Will it test 5.1 or 7.1 surround sound?

No. This page is limited to the controlled channels it explicitly presents and does not verify a multi-channel setup.

Why must I click Play?

Browsers normally block autoplay with sound. A click also lets you set a safe volume before audio begins.
