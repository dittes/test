Choose the task, not a label

Remembering a sequence is different from recognizing a word. Hitting a target combines seeing, moving and clicking; a reaction test keeps the target still. Typing adds a familiar skill and a speed–accuracy trade-off. A single score cannot stand in for all of these.

Make comparisons useful

Keep the same device, input method and browser size. Learn the rules before treating a run as a reference, and take breaks when you feel tired. Practice can improve your performance at the game; it does not establish a change in general intelligence.

What these games are not

These are informal online brain games, not validated cognitive assessments or medical screenings. We do not assign an IQ, a brain age or a percentile. Scores stay in page memory and disappear on reset or navigation.

Other simple games worth exploring

A visual memory grid would test locations without sequence order. A colour-word challenge could ask you to select ink colour rather than read the word. Those are possible future additions; the six playable tests above are available now.
